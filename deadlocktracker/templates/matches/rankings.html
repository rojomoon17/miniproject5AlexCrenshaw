How the ranking works

A plain win rate is misleading on small samples (win your only game on a hero and it reads 100%) and treats a game from months ago the same as last night's. Ratings fix both, the same way for every player, whether you've played 15 games or 1,500:

  1. Recent games count more. A game {{ half_life_days }} days older than your latest game counts half as much, {{ half_life_days * 2 }} days older a quarter. Age is measured from your latest game, not today's date, so taking a break doesn't erase your history. W-L records and game counts still include every game.
  2. Each rating starts from the level above it. Your average starts at 50%, which is what matchmaking aims every player at, and moves to your real average as your games add up ({{ baseline_prior_games }} games of pull towards 50%). Each hero then starts from your average, and each lane or matchup from your rating on that hero. Your games are added on top.
    Your average, step by step: {% if average_steps %} your record is {{ average_steps['wins'] }}-{{ average_steps['losses'] }} ({{ average_steps['plain'] }}%); weighting recent games more gives {{ average_steps['weighted'] }}% across {{ average_steps['eff_games'] }} "effective" games; the pull towards 50% then makes it {{ baseline }}%. {% if (average_steps['weighted'] - baseline)|abs >= 0.5 %}The fewer games you have, the bigger that last step.{% else %}With this many games the pull changes almost nothing.{% endif %} {% else %} with no games logged yet it's 50%. After your first win it becomes (1 + {{ baseline_prior_games }} × 50%) ÷ (1 + {{ baseline_prior_games }}) ≈ {{ (100 * (1 + baseline_prior_games * 0.5) / (1 + baseline_prior_games))|round(1) }}%, not 100%, and it keeps moving towards your real win rate as you play. {% endif %}
  3. The data decides how strong that starting point is. If your heroes' win rates differ by more than luck would explain, the differences are real and the starting point counts for little; if the spread looks like luck, it counts for more. This is worked out from your own games each time (between {{ k_min }} and {{ k_max }} "starting games"). {% if average_steps %}For your heroes right now it's {{ prior }}.{% else %}Until there are enough heroes to measure the spread it uses {{ prior }}.{% endif %}
  4. Every rating comes with a range. The bar shows where your true win rate probably is ({{ range_percent }}% likely). {{ 'With your numbers' if average_steps else 'Starting from 50%' }}, a 1-0 record rates {{ examples['one_win']['rating'] }}% ({{ examples['one_win']['low']|round|int }}–{{ examples['one_win']['high']|round|int }}%), 6-1 rates {{ examples['six_one']['rating'] }}% ({{ examples['six_one']['low']|round|int }}–{{ examples['six_one']['high']|round|int }}%) and 20-10 rates {{ examples['thirty']['rating'] }}% ({{ examples['thirty']['low']|round|int }}–{{ examples['thirty']['high']|round|int }}%): more games, narrower range.
  5. Only heroes with {{ min_ranked_games }}+ games, enough of them recent, are ranked. A hero with one or two games is rated "probably about average", which would otherwise outrank any hero proven slightly below average. The same goes for a hero whose games are mostly months old: they count for so little that its rating falls back towards your average. So a ranked hero's games must also count as at least {{ min_ranked_effective }} after weighting, which makes its own games at least a quarter of its rating, while a hero you play about twice a month still qualifies. Other heroes are listed under "Not enough games yet". Your Stats page shows how every rating is built.

On a hero page, the best lanes and matchups are ordered by the low end of their range and the toughest by the high end, so the ones we're surest about come first, and a hero needs at least {{ min_list_games }} games with or against you to be listed.

These settings were chosen by testing on many different kinds of player (newcomers, one-tricks, flex players, strong and struggling players, from 15 to 1,500 games). Across all of them, ratings were closer to the true win rate than the plain win rate was, and the true win rate fell inside the {{ range_percent }}% range 79–91% of the time.
